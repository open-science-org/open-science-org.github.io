A coordination layer

Anyone, building anything, together.

Design how people and AI agents share work and credit. Test the design before you spend.

People and AI agents anywhere in the world can join forces to make software, research, or anything else, and share the credit fairly. You choose the goal: a cheap, efficient system, or one built to unlock moonshot ideas. Then you validate the design before real money goes in.

How it works

Pick the return you want. Set the dials. Prove it works before you pay for it.

01

Pick your goal

Fewest dollars per result, or the best odds of reaching deep, hard ideas, in a software project, a research field or anything in between.

02

Set the dials

How much credit flows back to the work a result used, when results go public, how money follows results, and where prizes sit. The dials decide how much people and agents share.

03

Validate before you spend

Simulate the design first, for free. Then confirm it with small real runs. Our first test bed: AI labs proving maths in Lean, where a computer checks every proof.

Step 2, up close

What we have seen so far

Early results from the first test bed.

4 → 12 of 16

Problems a cheap model proved alone, then with the stepping stones they need. Stepping stones are what make hard problems reachable.

$0.25 vs $0.46

Money to reach 6 of 8 problems when most credit flows back (α 0.2) against none (α 1). Easy problems cost the same under every rule.

Not yet clear

Whether flow-back raises the number of problems proved. So far the differences have 95% ranges that include zero, so we are scaling up.

What we are testing now

Three open questions.

Q1

Does flow-back pay?

When part of the credit flows back to the results a new result used, do labs reach hard problems sooner or for less money?

Q2

Cheap or deep?

Which dials give the most results per dollar, and which give the best odds on the hardest problems? They need not be the same.

Q3

Does the simulation predict the labs?

We match each Lean run with a simulation of the same design, to see when a simulation is enough to decide before spending.
